# TIER 1 — The two things that can kill the submission

## 1.1 "This is a QR scanner with a database lookup. Where is the Physical AI?"

**This is the attack that ends the project, and right now you cannot answer it.**

You are in **Track 1: Physical AI**. Slide 7 claims five techniques working
together:

| Slide 7 claims | Actually in the repo |
| --- | --- |
| Vision-Language Models | ❌ nothing |
| Document Intelligence | ❌ nothing — seed data is hand-generated by a Python script |
| Forecasting & Optimization | ❌ `SELECT COUNT(*) ... WHERE count >= 2` |
| Agentic Systems | ❌ no agent, no tool loop, no planning |
| Construction-Specific AI / RAG | ❌ no retrieval, no embeddings, no documents |

**Zero of five are implemented.** A judge who opens the repo — and on a technical

rewrites a sentence. If they compare that to slide 7, you are not just weak on
AI, you look like you overclaimed. That is far worse than being simple and honest.

**And here is the uncomfortable part:** the design decision I have been
celebrating throughout — *"the verdict is a database join, not a model output"* —
is *also* the thing that makes this attack land hardest. It is genuinely good
engineering. It is also, on a Physical AI track, an argument that your product
contains no AI on its critical path. You cannot lead with "no model decides
anything" and also claim five AI techniques. Pick one story.

**Three ways out, in order of cost:**

**(a) Build the VLM nameplate reader. ~4 hours, cloud API, and it is the honest fix.**
No QR present → photograph the equipment nameplate → a vision model reads the
model number and serial off it → that feeds the same deterministic engine. This
gives you a real model doing real perception on a real physical object, which is
*exactly* what Physical AI means. It also completes the fallback ladder you
already claim in the README. **This is the single highest-value four hours left
in the project.** It was P2; promote it to P0.

**(b) Rewrite slide 7 to say what is built and what is next.** Free, and it
converts a credibility risk into a maturity signal — but you are then a team
with no AI on a Physical AI track.

**(c) Reframe the thesis:** "the AI is in perception and memory; the compliance
ruling is deliberately not AI, because a hallucinated compliance ruling is a
safety incident." This is a strong argument, **but only if (a) exists.** Without
a perception layer there is nothing on the AI side of the line.

Do (a) and (c) together. Doing (b) alone is surrender.

## 1.2 The memory feature does not update. Your flagship demo breaks on camera.

**Verified.** `App.tsx` loads the record into memory once, at mount (line 51).
`commitNcr()` writes the new NCR to SQLite — but nothing reloads `snapshot`.

So the sequence in your own video plan:

> Worker A installs the wrong part → NCR raised → Worker B scans the same SKU →
> "this has now happened 4 times"

**will show 3, not 4.** The count is frozen at whatever it was when the app
launched. The entire "institutional memory that compounds" story — slide 6, your
strongest differentiator — visibly does not work if anyone films it in one take.

You would very likely have discovered this at 2am on shoot night.

**Fix — one line**, after `commitNcr` and `recordVerifiedInstall` in `App.tsx`:
```ts
setSnapshot(await loadSnapshot());
```

# TIER 2 — Breaks the moment there are two phones

Slide 6 is *"Mon: installed wrong → Wed: another worker, same mistake."* Two
workers. Two phones. Both of the following break in that exact scenario.

## 2.1 NCR IDs collide across phones — silent data loss

```ts
// db.ts:162
return `NCR-${String(38 + (row?.n ?? 0)).padStart(4, '0')}`;  // COUNT(*) of LOCAL ncrs
```
Two phones each holding 5 local NCRs both mint `NCR-0043`. The server then does:
```js
db.ncrs = db.ncrs.filter(n => n.id !== payload.id).concat(payload)
```
Phone B's NCR **overwrites** Phone A's. A real nonconformance disappears with no
error anywhere. On a QA system, that is the worst possible failure class — worse
than crashing, because nobody finds out.

**Fix:** make the ID globally unique at creation.
`NCR-${deviceId}-${Date.now().toString(36)}`, or a UUID, with a human-readable
sequence assigned by the server on receipt.

## 2.2 Idempotency keys collide across phones — silent data loss

```ts
'Idempotency-Key': `outbox-${row.id}`   // row.id = local AUTOINCREMENT from 1
```
Every phone's first ever sync sends `outbox-1`. The server sees `outbox-1`
already in `db.seen`, returns `{ok:true, deduped:true}`, and **throws the write
away.** The phone marks it synced. Both sides believe it worked.

The mechanism I added specifically to protect data integrity is currently a
data-loss bug. It is correct for one device and actively harmful for two.

**Fix:** `Idempotency-Key: ${deviceId}-${row.id}`. Generate `deviceId` once at
first launch and store it in `meta`.

# TIER 3 — Will bite you on shoot day

## 3.1 Film on the wrong date and every verdict says ADVISORY

`witness_seed.json` hardcodes `record_synced_at: "2026-08-08T06:40:00Z"`.
Staleness threshold is 24h. **Verified:** filming on 20 Aug gives

> `authority=ADVISORY` — *"...last synced 290 hours ago, so treat this as advisory."*

Your hero shot needs `BINDING`. Every take after 9 Aug is silently degraded, and
the app is *correct* to do it, which is why you will not suspect a bug.

**Fix:** regenerate the seed on shoot morning (`python tools/make_seed.py` after
changing the timestamp to `now`), or have `ensureSeeded()` stamp
`record_synced_at` at install time.

## 3.2 Clock skew defeats staleness entirely

```ts
return Math.max(0, (b - a) / 36e5);   // negative age clamped to 0
```
**Verified:** a phone whose date is set earlier than the record's sync time
reports `ageHours=0, stale=false, authority=BINDING` — a record from the
*future* reads as perfectly fresh.

You have been calling stale-cache "the most dangerous failure mode in the
system." Your defence against it can be disabled by a wrong phone clock, which
is common on cheap site hardware. A judge who asks "what if the phone's clock is
wrong?" gets a bad answer.

**Fix:** treat negative age as *maximally* stale, not zero. Fail closed:
```ts
if (b < a) return Number.POSITIVE_INFINITY;
```

## 3.3 The airplane-mode shot is unreliable on Expo Go

`SETUP.md` step 5 tells you to film the offline sequence on Expo Go. Expo Go
loads its bundle from Metro over wifi. Once loaded it runs from memory, so it
*usually* survives — but any reconnect or reload gives you a red "disconnected
from Metro" banner mid-take.

That fifteen seconds is one of your best assets. **Build the standalone APK
before you film it.** Move Path B earlier.

## 3.4 Memory counts repeat scans of the same physical unit

**Verified:** scan one wrong part, confirm the NCR, scan it again, confirm again
— three times over — and Witness reports *"GT-12 has been confused here 6 times
before"* when there were **4 distinct units** involved and, really, one incident.

`memoryFor()` counts NCR rows, not distinct serials or distinct incidents. During
rehearsal you will scan `SN-4471` dozens of times. The count inflates every
single time. It will be absurd by shoot day, and if a judge notices the number
climbing on repeat scans, the "institutional memory" claim looks fake.

**Fix:** count distinct serials, or dedupe by `(serial, day)`.

# TIER 4 — Credibility when someone opens the repo

## 4.1 A test that asserts nothing

```ts
test('does not read the ambient clock', () => {
  (Date as any).now = () => 0;      // ...but hoursBetween uses Date.parse, not Date.now
```
`resolve()` never calls `Date.now()`, so this passes trivially and proves
nothing. It is the kind of test that looks rigorous and is theatre. **31 passing
tests** is a number you have been quoting; one of them is decorative. Fix it or
delete it — a reviewer who spots it will discount the other thirty.

## 4.2 TypeScript will not type-check

`resolve.ts` imports `'./types.ts'` with an explicit extension. That requires
`allowImportingTsExtensions: true` in `tsconfig.json`, which is not set.
Metro and `node --test` both tolerate it; `tsc` and VS Code will not. Anyone who
opens this in an editor sees red squiggles across the engine — the file you most
want them to admire.

**Fix:** add `"allowImportingTsExtensions": true, "noEmit": true` to
`tsconfig.json`.

## 4.3 No way to reset the phone's data

`ensureSeeded(force)` supports a force reseed. Nothing ever calls it with `true`,
and there is no UI for it. Between takes your only option is Android Settings →
clear app storage. You will be doing this a lot, under time pressure, and it also
wipes the Expo Go bundle.

**Fix:** a long-press on the zone label that calls `ensureSeeded(true)`. Ten minutes.

## 4.4 Dashboard baseline undercuts your own story

Seeded installs already show ~75–100% field-verified before Witness does
anything. The pitch is that Witness *creates* coverage that does not exist today.
Your opening screen says coverage is already excellent.

**Fix:** seed most installs as unverified so the number visibly climbs as you scan.

## 4.5 Minor, but real
- `db.seen` on the server grows unbounded; no eviction.
- `store.json` is rewritten whole on every request — concurrent writes can corrupt it.
- Camera permission denial shows an infinite spinner with no error state or retry.
- `App.tsx` reads zones from the bundled JSON while everything else reads SQLite —
  two sources of truth for the same data.

## The honest summary

The engineering is genuinely good — the offline-first architecture, the
deterministic safety path, the graceful unknown states, and the fail-closed
staleness design are better than most hackathon submissions will manage, and the
phone-first reframe is a stronger adoption story than the glasses ever were.

But: **there is no AI in an AI competition**, the **memory feature does not
actually update**, and **two of the integrity mechanisms cause data loss with two
phones** — the exact scenario the pitch is built on.

None of that is fatal today. All of it is fatal on Friday.
